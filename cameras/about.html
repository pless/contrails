What this is

A per-camera view of the contrailCameras registry: public sky cameras that update at least about once a minute and that we are calibrating (position, pointing, field of view, lens distortion) so that contrails and clouds seen in them can be placed in the sky.

Where the data comes from

registryThe contrailCameras Google Sheet, tab cameras, one row per camera. The page reads the sheet directly when it is shared as “anyone with the link can view”; otherwise it falls back to the snapshot built into the site (the badge in the header says which one you are looking at).
camera_idws- + the first 8 hex characters of SHA-1 of the normalized image URL. Minted once and never renumbered; it is also column A of the WorldScope sheet and the name of the camera's Drive folder.
current imageFetched from the camera's own image URL each time the page loads (and every 60 s while auto-refresh is on). Cameras that only serve plain http:// are loaded through the images.weserv.nl proxy because this site is served over https. YouTube feeds are embedded. Időkép and Viaero cameras are not shown here (their terms do not allow it): the page links to the camera's own page instead, and they are not in the hourly capture.
stored imagesFrames saved for calibration in Google Drive under Worldscope/cameras/<camera_id>/images/. An hourly job (tools/capture_hourly.py, run by launchd on Robert's Mac) saves one validated frame per live camera there, in shuffled order and with per-host rate limits; YouTube streams get one frame through yt-dlp + ffmpeg. The montage shows thumbnails made at build time by tools/build_dashboard.py (the newest 6 per curated camera, the newest 1 for Pool A and AllSky7 rows); each links to the file in Drive when its id is known. The “captured” column in the list is the time of the last saved frame.
constraints / calibrationconstraints.json (priors and hand-clicked evidence) and calibration.json (solver output) live next to images/ in the camera's Drive folder.

Status vocabulary

candidatelive and fast (≥ 1 image/min), nobody has started on it yet
slowlive but updates less than once a minute: fine for calibration, not for cloud tracking
deadthe image URL fails (date in parentheses)
stalethe URL works but the image is old
needs locationno coordinates yet
in progresssomeone is collecting evidence / solving
calibrated (T1–T4)solved; the tier says how well: T1 coarse (~5°), T2 horizon and landmarks (~0.5°), T3 celestial (a few pixels), T4 independently validated
skip: reasonnot worth pursuing

The first word is the machine-readable part; anything after it is evidence. Cadence is a fixed property of a camera, measured once with a two-look check (two requests about 62 s apart), not monitored.

Verification

The verification page shows the evidence behind each ALERTCalifornia camera marked calibrated. These cameras turn, so what is checked is the pointing the network publishes (pan, tilt, field of view) for the frame in hand. Two checks, independent of each other:

terrain skylineThe camera position is known, so the skyline it should see can be computed from an elevation model (AWS Terrain Tiles, out to 150 km, with earth curvature and refraction). The skyline is also found in up to eight stored frames. Pan, tilt, roll and focal length are then fitted so the computed skyline lands on the found one. A fit counts when the median miss is at most 3 pixels and at least 85% of the columns agree. A flat horizon cannot pin pan or focal length, so there only tilt and roll are fitted. tools/verify_skyline.py
sunFor every stored frame where the published pointing puts the sun inside the picture, the saturated sun is found and compared with where it should be. consistent means within 2° in azimuth and 4° in elevation; offset means farther than that in at least two frames that agree; unclear means farther but only once or inconsistently. tools/verify_sun.py
statusA camera is marked calibrated when it has a clean skyline fit or a consistent sun; the status text in the sheet says which, with the numbers. It is held as in progress when the skyline fit asks for a pan change above 1.5° that nothing else confirms, or when the two checks disagree.

The pictures and numbers on that page are made by python tools/build_verify_page.py --out ~/githubCloneContrails/contrails/cameras, run after either check has new results.

Rebuilding the site

python tools/build_dashboard.py data/contrailCameras/contrailCameras.csv \
    --out ~/githubCloneContrails/contrails/cameras \
    --images data/contrailCameras/seeds \
    --images "$HOME/Library/CloudStorage/GoogleDrive-…/My Drive/Worldscope/cameras" \
    --images ~/contrailCapture/spool --captures ~/contrailCapture \
    --drive-uploads data/contrailCameras/drive_uploads.csv \
    --max-per-camera 6 --max-pool-a 1 \
    --snapshot-label "contrailCameras sheet, exported YYYY-MM-DD"

Then commit and push the cameras/ directory of the contrails repository.
